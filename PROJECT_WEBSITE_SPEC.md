## 1. A0 Project Brief

**Project purpose:** Rebuild the root creative experience of markkrizsan.com to sell and showcase Mark Krizsan as a photographer, filmmaker and creative director at or above the quality of the existing /web experience, without cloning /web.

**Primary page objective:** Turn visual taste into commercial trust and qualified project inquiries.

**Five-second belief:** Mark has a real eye.

**Ten-second proof sequence:** EYE -> CRAFT -> RANGE.

**Deeper proof sequence:** DIRECTION -> COMMERCIAL CLARITY -> HIRE.

**Primary action:** Start a project.

**Secondary action:** View the work.

**Core promise:** You hire Mark when how the person, brand, place, product or story is perceived matters enough to get right.

**Supporting friction reducer:** Bring the objective. You do not need a finished treatment.

## 2. Primary Buyer

A decision-maker responsible for a public-facing person, brand, product, place, campaign, release or cultural project where visual perception materially affects how it is received.

Typical roles may include founder, artist/talent, brand owner, creative lead, marketing lead or producer. Role is secondary to situation.

**Situation:** Something important is being introduced, repositioned, promoted or documented, but the visual world is unclear, inconsistent, generic, outdated or insufficient for the stakes.

**User job:** When something important needs to be seen correctly, the buyer needs a creative partner who can define and execute the visual language so the finished work creates the intended perception rather than merely producing more content.

**Desired progress:** Move from “we need content/visuals” to a coherent visual world that makes the subject easier to notice, understand, desire, remember or trust.

**Decision enabled:** Is Mark the right person to trust with how this will be perceived?

**Top frictions:** taste risk; fit/adaptability risk; creative-direction ambiguity; execution risk; commercial-objective risk; engagement ambiguity.

**Required beliefs:**
1. Mark has exceptional visual judgment.
2. His taste adapts to the assignment rather than imposing one house look.
3. He can originate the visual idea, not only execute a shot list.
4. He can carry the idea into finished photography and/or film.
5. He understands commercial/cultural objectives behind the visuals.
6. The buyer does not need a finished treatment before contact.
7. Getting the perception right is worth investing in.

## 4. Offer Architecture

One integrated creative practice with three engagement modes:

**Photography:** when one frame or a body of images needs to establish the perception.

**Film:** when movement, voice, rhythm and atmosphere are essential to the idea.

**Creative Direction:** when the visual world itself needs defining before production begins.

Projects may include one, two or all three. The website must not force buyers to diagnose the production solution before inquiry.

## 5. Homepage Narrative

### ACT 01 — THE EYE
**Job:** Create immediate desire and establish category.  
**Primary proof:** One hero-tier real still or restrained motion asset.  
**Copy:** Category line + short headline + one positioning sentence.  
**Actions:** View work / Start a project.  
**Reject:** collage by default, long service copy, multiple competing CTAs, motion required for comprehension.

### ACT 02 — SELECTED WORLDS
**Job:** Prove craft, range and adaptability through three real projects.  
**World 01:** Represents work Mark most wants more of.  
**World 02:** Proves range without loss of authorship.  
**World 03:** Proves another commercially useful capability/dimension.  
**Metadata:** real project/client/subject where permitted, exact role, medium, year/location only when useful.  
**Reject:** generic Presence/Campaign/Story category framing.

### ACT 03 — DIRECTOR'S CUT
**Job:** Prove creative authorship.  
**Evidence sequence:** IDEA -> LANGUAGE -> FRAME -> MOTION -> FINAL.  
**Requirement:** chosen project must contain meaningful pre-production/decision evidence, not merely a moodboard plus finished images.  
**Desktop:** interactive stage if justified.  
**Mobile:** vertical evidence chapters.  
**Reduced motion:** instant state changes.  
**Reject:** WebGL, scroll hijacking, drag-only behavior, custom cursor, novelty without evidence value.

### ACT 04 — THE FIELD
**Job:** Prove depth and abundance.  
**Target:** 12–18 maximum selected assets.  
**Composition:** art-directed sequence of still and motion with variable scale/aspect ratio; not masonry or equal cards.  
**Copy:** nearly none.

### ACT 05 — MARK / THE PRACTICE
**Job:** Connect the eye to the person and reduce relationship risk.  
**Media:** current color portrait.  
**Copy target:** 60–90 words.  
**Must answer:** who is Mark; how does he think; why trust him with the assignment.  
**Practical metadata:** Orange County / Los Angeles; available for travel; Photography / Film / Direction.

### ACT 06 — COMMISSION
**Job:** Make engagement concrete and convert the right buyer.  
**Content:** three engagement modes, concise fit filter, inquiry path.  
**Self-qualification language:** may include “we’ll probably work well together if…” only as a late-stage fit filter, not primary positioning.  
**Pricing:** unresolved until qualification value vs anchoring risk is explicitly decided.

## 6. Copy Rules

**Homepage target:** approximately 300–450 meaningful words excluding form/legal/footer.

Copy must be specific, confident, concise, speakable and commercial without agency mush.

Use the work as the noun; use copy to explain the decision.

Avoid: elevate, seamless, unlock, cutting-edge, generic storytelling claims, overuse of “not just X but Y,” paragraphs narrating visible imagery, unexplained superlatives.

Delete copy when removal leaves comprehension unchanged.

## 7. Proof Architecture

Primary proof is real work.

Additional proof may include named clients, real testimonials, published usage, real campaign context, press, credentials or outcomes only when specific, true, relevant and verifiable.

Never fabricate proof.

For each project record exact authorship: concept, direction, photography, cinematography, editing, styling/location/props, production, etc., only where true.

## 8. Asset Selection Criteria

Every candidate asset is evaluated on:
- impact
- craft
- taste
- authorship clarity
- commercial direction
- distinctiveness
- currency
- usage rights
- web viability

Two independent judgments are required:
**QUALITY:** Is this among Mark’s best current work?  
**DIRECTION:** Does showing this attract more of the work Mark wants next?

Final classifications:
- Hero Tier
- Selected World / Case-study Tier
- Supporting / Field
- Do Not Use

Do not lock final layout before Hero Tier, three Selected Worlds and Director’s Cut candidate are known.

## 9. IA / Route Direction

Current generic project taxonomy (/projects/presence, /campaign, /story) is provisional legacy architecture.

Preferred future case-study route: `/work/[actual-project]/`.

A project earns a dedicated route only when it can sustain real context, enough finished work, exact role and meaningful creative decisions. Preferred additional evidence: objective, pre-production, motion, real usage/context, outcome/testimonial.

Old routes require redirect/retention decision before launch.

`/web` remains a sibling offer and must remain intact.

## 10. Visual System

**Relationship to /web:** same author, different experience.

Preserve:
- warm bone / near-black / signal red world
- disciplined typography
- generous exterior breathing room
- sharp hierarchy
- precision
- plain right/down arrows
- restrained accent usage
- mobile-specific thinking

Evolve:
- more media dominance
- more cinematic stages
- variable aspect ratios
- less service-page geometry
- less text
- stronger sequencing
- darker media stages where useful
- greater visual tempo variation

**Design-language mix:** Contemporary Editorial / Cinematic Portfolio (dominant) + Swiss/Modernist discipline (secondary) + restrained raw cultural/artist energy (disruptor).

**Creative intensity:** 5/7 Art-directed to Experimental, without sacrificing orientation.

## 11. Interaction / Motion

**Motion budget:** restrained cinematic.

Allowed jobs: orientation, hierarchy, reveal, sequence, feedback, media demonstration, atmosphere.

One major signature interaction maximum on homepage, currently Director’s Cut if content earns it.

Hover is supplementary only. Touch and keyboard equivalents are required. Reduced-motion equivalents are required. Motion must not delay access to content.

## 12. Responsive / Mobile

Responsive design preserves value, not desktop geometry.

Test minimum widths: 320, 375, 390, 768, 1024, 1440, 1920.

Hard requirements:
- zero accidental horizontal overflow
- no clipped hero type
- no critical crop loss
- no essential hover
- usable touch targets/controls
- video controls cannot obscure important footage
- captions remain attached to correct media
- accent meaning preserved across breakpoints
- forms remain usable with software keyboard

Likely transformations:
- cinematic desktop hero -> dedicated mobile crop/media
- editorial world layouts -> sequential vertical compositions
- Director’s Cut stage -> vertical evidence chapters
- hover previews -> tap/persistent state
- wide film -> complete meaningful frame/contain where needed
- side metadata -> attached captions

## 13. Engineering

**Architecture:** static HTML/CSS + small vanilla JS unless a requirement proves a stronger runtime is necessary.

**Principle:** least powerful sufficient technology.

**Dependencies:** keep minimal.

**Forms:** existing Formspree infrastructure may be reused after final inquiry design is approved.

**Progressive enhancement:** content and primary action remain available without optional motion.

## 14. Media Performance

No video belongs on the critical path unless it creates materially more value than a still.

Hero should default to a strong poster/still and progressively enhance into motion when appropriate.

Use responsive modern image formats, explicit dimensions/aspect ratios, appropriate srcset/sizes, lazy secondary media, strong poster frames, deliberate preload/fetchpriority, offscreen video pause/load behavior where practical.

## 15. Performance / Accessibility / Semantics

**Accessibility target:** WCAG 2.2 AA.

**Core Web Vitals quality targets:**
- LCP <= 2.5 s
- INP <= 200 ms
- CLS <= 0.1

Required:
- semantic landmarks
- one meaningful H1
- logical heading order
- meaningful alt text
- visible focus
- keyboard-operable custom interactions
- labeled forms/errors
- reduced-motion support
- sufficient contrast
- no autoplay audio
- media alternatives/captions/transcripts where required
- no console errors
- crawl/index intent explicit

## 16. Conversion

## 19. Permanent Rules / Exclusions

- No diagonal arrow glyphs.
- Do not clutter portfolio imagery with explanatory overlays.
- Real work before generated imagery.
- No stale portfolio assets when stronger current work exists.
- Mobile is intentionally composed.
- Important brand meaning survives mobile.
- Do not crop proof so aggressively it cannot be understood.
- Founder portraits remain color unless a concept specifically earns another treatment.
- Motion supports content; it does not become the content.
- Taste first, explanation second.
- Do not turn every page into the same template.
- Refine/subtract once architecture works.
- No generic masonry.
- No giant FAQ.
- No giant Method chapter.
- No separate Photo/Video essays on homepage.
- No fake showreel requirement.
- No gratuitous WebGL/custom cursor/scroll hijacking.
